I visited Meghalaya with my parents during the monsoon. At first, we were sceptical about travelling in the rain,
afraid that we would spend most of our time confined indoors. Instead,
the trip turned out to be one of the best vacations we had ever taken.

Meghalaya during monsoon felt almost otherworldly. The rivers ran clear, waterfalls heavied over the hills, and forests bloomed lush and alive.
A familiar cold breeze moved through the air, evoking a long lost nostalgia of anticipation,
dreamy cloudscapes, and liminal calmness.

> “The whole of nature is a metaphor of the human mind.”
>
> *Ralph Waldo Emerson, Nature*

I wonder if there is some deep connection between human psychology and nature. Over and above their beauty,
these photographs depict subjects at ease, free from the burdens
of daily life and existing in their most natural form.

We could sense a certain
peace and friendliness in the people, and it made us wonder if stress and frustration
are children of the contemporary lifestyle. These photographs reflect an idea of time when people are free from the
acts of doing and just simply exist in unison with the ecosystem around them.

> Humans communicate through words, but nature speaks in silence. It emphasises a poetic drama.

It lives in the space where two things meet and briefly transform one another.
Water finds its voice as it falls into a reservoir, wind turns into music as it
passes through the leaves, clouds collide and erupt into thunder
and raindrops tiptoe upon the soil.

Even the most ordinary moments are beautiful and sometimes you can just sit and take it all in silently in awe.
